# Statistical validation and real-world comparison

## Purpose and scope

The validation notebooks answer two separate questions:

1. **OULAD validation:** does the latest OULAD presentation differ from the
   earlier presentations used as development context, and how do its
   early-warning measurements compare with an authorised real-world dataset?
2. **Synthetic validation:** are the generated planner records internally
   coherent and statistically associated with the simulated outcome, and how
   different are their observable engagement measurements from real data?

The notebooks are:

- [`11_oulad_statistical_validation.ipynb`](../notebooks/11_oulad_statistical_validation.ipynb)
- [`12_synthetic_statistical_validation.ipynb`](../notebooks/12_synthetic_statistical_validation.ipynb)

The reusable tests are implemented in
[`src/validation/statistical.py`](../src/validation/statistical.py), and the
reports are generated by [`pipeline/statistical_validation.py`](../pipeline/statistical_validation.py).

## Statistical protocol

The analysis uses an analysis-specific significance level of `alpha = 0.05`.
Within each family of related tests, p-values are adjusted with the
Holm-Bonferroni procedure. This reduces the chance of presenting a long list
of unadjusted tests as independent evidence.

### Numeric distributions

For each declared numeric feature, the notebooks report:

- mean, median, standard deviation, and quartiles for context;
- the two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic and p-value for a general
  distributional difference;
- the two-sided Mann-Whitney U p-value as a rank/location comparison; and
- Cliff's delta, whose sign indicates whether the comparison cohort tends to
  have larger values.

The tests do not require normally distributed measurements. With large samples,
very small practical differences can still produce small p-values, so medians,
quartiles, and effect sizes must be read with the hypothesis-test result.

### Categorical distributions

Categorical composition is compared using a chi-square test of independence.
Cramer's V is reported as an effect-size measure. The test asks whether cohort
membership and the observed category are independent; it does not explain why
the cohorts differ.

### Binary risk rates

Risk prevalence is compared with a two-row contingency table and a chi-square
test. The rate difference is reported explicitly. A rate difference is a
population-composition diagnostic, not a causal effect and not evidence that
one institution is better or worse.

### Feature-to-risk association

For the synthetic dataset, numeric planner features are compared between
`risk_label = 0` and `risk_label = 1` with the same rank-based procedure. The
result documents dependence in the generated records. It must not be described
as a causal relationship because the simulator may have generated both the
feature and the outcome from shared latent rules.

## Internal OULAD validation

OULAD uses the latest presentation (`2014J`) as the comparison cohort and the
earlier presentations as the reference cohort. The analysis tests declared
static fields, risk prevalence, and cutoff-specific engagement aggregates.
This is a temporal stability audit for the evaluation population; it is not a
claim that the latest presentation is an independent university population.

The model-metric table is read from the leakage-safe early-warning report. It
is compared with the KU Leuven table descriptively at days 30, 60, and 90.
Because each dataset contributes one aggregate score per cutoff, those metric
differences do not receive an artificial p-value.

## Internal synthetic validation

The synthetic notebook first records the relational-integrity report. It then
tests:

- numeric planner-feature associations with the simulated risk label;
- numeric distribution differences between student-disjoint train and test
  cohorts; and
- first-term versus last-term risk prevalence.

The student-disjoint split is an audit of cohort composition, not a replacement
for the baseline model's locked test evaluation. A non-significant result does
not prove realism or representativeness.

## Real-world comparison

The authorised KU Leuven Open Data, Private Learners release is the primary
external reference. OULAD is also used as a real educational-data reference
for the synthetic track. The common comparison contract is deliberately narrow:

| Common field | OULAD mapping | Synthetic mapping | KU Leuven mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| `activity_count` | VLE clicks | study-session rows | LMS action records |
| `active_day_count` | distinct VLE dates | distinct study dates | distinct LMS activity dates |
| `content_count` | distinct VLE sites | distinct topic IDs | distinct content IDs |
| `session_count` | active-day proxy | distinct study sessions | distinct LMS sessions |
| `mean_daily_activity` | activity / active days | activity / active days | activity / active days |

This mapping tests measurement compatibility, not semantic identity. KU Leuven
and OULAD do not contain the synthetic planner's sleep, break, screen-time,
wellbeing, CGPA, or Indian-university constructs. A distribution mismatch in
the common engagement fields therefore does not by itself invalidate those
planner attributes; it shows that they remain unvalidated development
features.

## Reproduction commands

```powershell
\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pipeline.statistical_validation oulad `
  --data-dir data/processed/oulad `
  --kuleuven-path data/raw/external/kuleuven/dataset.zip `
  --output artifacts/validation/oulad_statistical_validation.json

\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pipeline.statistical_validation synthetic `
  --data-dir data/raw/synthetic `
  --oulad-dir data/processed/oulad `
  --kuleuven-path data/raw/external/kuleuven/dataset.zip `
  --output artifacts/validation/synthetic_statistical_validation.json
```

The reports are generated from local data and are not fabricated. They must be
regenerated after changing the source data, feature contract, statistical
library version, or cutoffs.

The same commands are registered as the DVC stages
`oulad_statistical_validation` and `synthetic_statistical_validation`. Their
DVC-owned outputs are written under `artifacts/validation/`. Their producing
commands and content hashes are recorded in `dvc.lock`, while executed notebook
outputs retain the reviewed plots and formal interpretation.

```powershell
dvc repro oulad_statistical_validation synthetic_statistical_validation
```

`.github/workflows/data-validation.yml` restores the DagsHub DVC cache and
reproduces the model, fairness-integrity, and statistical-validation evidence
each week and on manual dispatch. It requires `DAGSHUB_USERNAME` and
`DAGSHUB_TOKEN` repository secrets. The workflow uploads reports and fails if
the committed DVC lock no longer reproduces. Ordinary pull-request CI validates
the DVC graph and statistical functions without downloading restricted data.

## Interpretation boundary

These reports establish measured distribution and association diagnostics under
the stated samples. They do not establish prospective intervention benefit,
fairness, calibration in deployment, population representativeness, or causal
effects. Any production decision still requires prospective institutional
validation, subgroup review, an approved intervention policy, and human
oversight.
